What we carry

A heritage made of ordinary, extraordinary things.

English heritage belongs to everyone who calls England home: families rooted for centuries in a Yorkshire dale, and families who arrived last year and made a street in Leicester or Bristol their own. Every one of those stories adds to the whole.

It lives in accents that change every few miles, in recipes scribbled on the back of envelopes, in allotments and seaside towns, and in the people who remember how it all used to be.

i.
Language

Words & voices

The language of Chaucer, Shakespeare and the folk song, and also of your nan’s sayings, your town’s slang and the dialect words that make strangers smile. Keep the phrases that sound like home.

ii.
Food

The family table

Sunday roasts and Yorkshire puddings, Cornish pasties, a proper pot of tea, the curry night that became a tradition. Food is how families say “you belong here.”

iii.
Art & craft

Made by hand

Landscape painting, choirs and brass bands, quilting, pottery, knitting patterns handed down. Creativity in every parlour and pub.

iv.
Places

Where it happened

The village green, the high street, the mill town, the seaside pier. Name the places that made your family who it is.

v.
Family stories

Keep the tales

How they met, where they worked, the war years, the move, the funny bits. Stories vanish quietly unless someone keeps them.

Like an oak, a family grows from its roots up.

The stories you keep today become the shade someone else sits in tomorrow.

Start this week

Five ways to honour your heritage.

Small, doable, and the kind of thing you’ll be glad you did.

1

Record a grandparent’s story

Ten minutes on your phone. Ask about their childhood, first job, and the best day they remember.

2

Cook the family recipe

Make the dish everyone asks for. Write it down properly and note who it came from.

3

Learn a phrase

A dialect word, an old saying, a line from a folk song or a toast. Pass it to a child.

4

Visit & trace

Walk the streets your family lived on, explore parish and public records, or sketch a family tree together.

5

Share it

Post a photo, recipe or story with #HonorYourHeritage and invite others to join in.

Over a cup of tea

Questions to ask before it’s too late.

  • What was your street like when you were young?
  • Who taught you to cook, and what did they make?
  • What did you do on Saturdays?
  • What’s a saying our family always used?
  • What do you hope we never forget?
