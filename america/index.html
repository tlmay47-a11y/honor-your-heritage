Our heritage

A nation founded on an idea, and built by the people who believed in it.

On July 4, 1776, the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence. Eleven years later, in the same Philadelphia hall, delegates signed a Constitution that began with three words: “We the People.” From farmers at Lexington and Concord to the pioneers, inventors and builders who followed, Americans have been writing this story ever since.

I.

The Revolution

1775–1783 · The fight for independence

It began with the “shot heard round the world” at Lexington and Concord in April 1775. Ordinary farmers, shopkeepers and craftsmen held on through the hard winter at Valley Forge and won at Saratoga and Yorktown. In 1783 the Treaty of Paris made it official: the United States was free and independent.

II.

The Declaration

July 4, 1776

Thomas Jefferson’s words, adopted by the Continental Congress, told the world why a people may govern themselves: “that all men are created equal” and are endowed with rights including “Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness.”

III.

The Constitution

Signed September 17, 1787

“We the People of the United States, in Order to form a more perfect Union…” A charter for self-government with checks and balances, still the foundation of the nation more than two centuries later.

IV.

The Bill of Rights

Ratified December 15, 1791

Ten amendments that protect freedom of religion, speech, the press and assembly, along with the right to a fair trial. Freedoms Americans still carry with them every day.

V.

What We Built

The builder’s spirit

Wagon trails and railroads, barns raised by neighbors, bridges, factories, inventions and a flag planted on the Moon. Generation after generation, Americans have turned freedom into homes, towns and dreams for their children.

Five simple acts

Five ways to honor your heritage.

No grand gesture required. Pick one this week. Small acts, repeated, are how a free people keeps its story alive.

  1. 01

    Read the founding wordsGo to the source

    Read the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution’s Preamble out loud with your family, maybe on the Fourth of July. They’re short, and they still stir the heart.

  2. 02

    Fly the flagWear it proudly

    Raise the Stars and Stripes on a holiday, learn its history, and teach a child how to fold it and treat it with respect.

  3. 03

    Visit where it happenedWalk the ground

    Independence Hall and the Liberty Bell, Lexington and Concord, Valley Forge, Yorktown, or the historic site and veterans’ memorial closest to home.

  4. 04

    Thank those who servedKeep faith with them

    Ask a veteran or an older relative about their service and their America. Record the story, and find the patriots and builders in your own family tree.

  5. 05

    Share itPass it on

    Post a photo, a family story or your favorite line from our founding documents with #HonorYourHeritage and invite others to share theirs. Liberty grows when it’s shared.

On the porch, at the table

Questions to ask this Fourth of July.

Ask the oldest person at the table. Listen longer than feels natural. Write down the names, places and dates, and the feelings too.

  • What does being an American mean to you?
  • Who in our family served our country, and what did they tell you?
  • What did our family build, grow or make with their own hands?
  • What did the Fourth of July look and sound like when you were young?
  • Which freedom are you most grateful for?
