Our heritage

A country of many beginnings, carried home in every family.

Whether your people are Cherokee or Lakota, came through Ellis Island or a Texas border town, survived the Middle Passage, crossed the Pacific, or arrived last year with two suitcases, your heritage is something you belong to and help write. Where you came from is worth remembering.

I.

Voices

Language & the way we speak

The languages your family has spoken here: Navajo and Ojibwe, Spanish and Tagalog, Yiddish and Gullah, Vietnamese, Arabic, Polish, Haitian Creole, and the English of your grandmother’s porch. The accents, the nicknames, the sayings only your family understands. Every word you keep is a thread back to someone who spoke it first.

II.

The Table

Food

Frybread and gumbo, tamales at Christmas, pierogi, collard greens, pho, jollof, a holiday pie with a secret. The dish your family insists only they make right is a history lesson you can taste.

III.

Art & Music

What our hands and voices make

Spirituals, blues and jazz, powwow drums, bluegrass fiddles, mariachi, quilts, beadwork, murals. Art isn’t only in museums; it lives in the hands that taught you to stitch, carve, sing or play.

IV.

Places

Where we’re from

Ancestral homelands, a farm in the Delta, a block in the Bronx, a reservation, a mill town, a port city where someone first stepped ashore. The places that shaped your family deserve to be named.

V.

Family Stories

The ones we tell

The journeys, the courage, the love stories, the hard years and the funny ones retold at every reunion. They live in memory, and memory needs keepers. That can be you.

Five simple acts

Five ways to honor your heritage.

No grand gesture required. Pick one this week. Small acts, repeated, are how heritage survives.

  1. 01

    Record their storiesPress record

    Sit with a grandparent or elder, press record on your phone, and ask: Where did our family come from? What did home look like? What do you want us to remember?

  2. 02

    Cook the family recipeWrite it down

    Find the dish that tastes like home. Cook it with someone, write the recipe down properly, and note who taught it to whom.

  3. 03

    Learn a word or a songKeep the language

    A greeting in an ancestral language, a lullaby, a hymn, a toast. Teach it to a child. Use it at the table.

  4. 04

    Visit & traceFollow the road back

    Visit a hometown, a homeland, a cemetery or a cultural center. Explore public archives and records, and build a simple family tree with the relatives who remember the most.

  5. 05

    Share itPass it on

    Post a photo, a recipe or a story with #HonorYourHeritage and invite others to share theirs. Heritage grows when it’s shared.

On the porch, at the table

Questions to ask while you still can.

Ask slowly. Listen longer than feels natural. Write down the names, places and dates, and the feelings too.

  • Where did our family come from, as far back as you know?
  • Who in our family was the first to arrive here, or who was always here?
  • What did your grandparents teach you that you still use?
  • What did holidays smell and sound like when you were young?
  • What do you hope we never forget?
